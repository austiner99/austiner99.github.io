Research

Teaching robots to catch
what humans throw.

I study how people coordinate when they throw to each other, and use it to build robots that can be real partners in dynamic, physical work, from disaster relief to flood barriers.

Three threads, one goal

01

Dyadic throwing biomechanics

How do two people coordinate a throw and a catch? Motion capture of 20 dyads reveals the strategies partners use, including how the pre-release swing acts as a signal to the catcher.

Motion captureStatisticsPython
02

Haptic human-robot collaboration

Motion cues can be felt as well as seen. I explore how haptic channels let a robot infer a human partner's intent and respond in sync, supporting shared control and cooperative manipulation.

HapticsIntent recognitionShared control
03

Dynamic manipulation for humanitarian robotics

Throwing is a fast way to move objects farther than an arm can reach. I'm working toward robots that throw and catch with people for disaster relief, flood response, and other time-critical tasks.

RoboticsTrajectory planningDisaster relief
